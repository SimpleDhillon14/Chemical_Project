Chemical Reaction Kinetics Analyzer

Analyze experimental concentration-time data using CRE graphical and mathematical methods. An interactive application for analyzing experimental concentration-time data using Integral and Differential methods, and specialized graphical methods for autocatalytic, reversible, and irreversible reactions — every equation, transformation, graph, slope, and intercept is shown step by step.

Method 1
Integral Method

Guess a reaction order n, integrate the rate law, and test the straight-line plot of CA1-n (or -ln CA) vs. t. Supports zero, first, second, third, and fractional orders via a systematic R² comparison.

Open Method →
Method 2
Differential Method

Numerically differentiate CA vs. t to obtain -dCA/dt, then plot log10(-dCA/dt) vs. log10(CA). Slope = order n, intercept = log10(k), exactly per Fig. 3.18 of the course notes.

Open Method →
Method 3
Autocatalytic Reaction

A + R → R + R. Uses the linear form ln[(M+XA)/(M(1-XA))] vs. t, where M = CR0/CA0, to extract the rate constant k.

Open Method →
Method 4
Reversible Reaction

A ⇌ R. Uses the equilibrium concentration CAe and the linear form -ln[(CA-CAe)/(CA0-CAe)] vs. t to find k₁ and k₂.

Open Method →
Method 5
Irreversible Reaction

A → R → S (series). Extracts k₁ from -ln(CA/CA0) vs. t, and optionally fits k₂ from CR(t) data, including tmax and CR,max.
